# Postcode Wars (ENDS) — the pitch

**A click-only crime RPG on a map of the UK.** You are one character. You start at
level 1 as a nobody in London and grind criminal work until you're level 100 and
the biggest boss in the country. Tap a city to travel there. Inside a city, pick a
borough and get a list of jobs. Tap a job, it resolves, you get money and XP, you
level up. Some jobs and events pop a **choice card** — text and 2–4 buttons — and
the option you tap decides what happens. Bigger cities mean bigger crime, bigger
money and bigger danger. You recruit crew, build an organisation, and eventually
fight other players' gangs asynchronously.

## The interaction model — Clash of Clans, not GTA

There is **no avatar you steer** and **no scene you walk through**. The character is
a portrait, a level, a stat block. The world is a stylised UK map with tappable
city nodes. A "district" is a screen with a list of jobs, not a place.

Every interaction is one of:
1. Tap a button
2. Tap a card in a list
3. Tap a node on the map
4. Tap one of 2–4 options on a choice card
5. Drag a slider / scroll a list

No aiming, timing bars, dodging, steering or lock-picking minigames. Outcomes are
**resolved by code and then animated** — never played by the player.

## The spine — an RPG character, levels 1→100

Level is the single progression backbone. XP comes from every job and event. Each
level grants a stat point and Energy, with a ceremony; milestone levels unlock
whole systems (5 = second city, 10 = crew + laundering, 25 = county lines, 50 =
firms, 75 = war, 100 = **Top Boy**). Rank titles (Wasteman → Top Boy) map onto
level bands.

## What survives from the old design

The fiction, slang, economy (dirty vs clean), crime content, county lines,
factions, ranks, dopamine architecture and compliance rules are all still the
source of truth. Only the *interaction format* changed — from a walkable real-time
world to a click-driven app.
